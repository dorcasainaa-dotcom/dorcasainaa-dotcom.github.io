Wider Health Determinants Dashboard
Southampton & Statistical Neighbours

Multi-page interactive dashboard analysing air pollution and child poverty indicators across Southampton and three statistical neighbours (Portsmouth, Bristol, Plymouth) covering 2014–2023. Built on OHID public health data with custom DAX measures, England average benchmarks, and a bespoke background UI.

Power BI DAX Microsoft Fabric OHID data Performance reporting Public health analytics
The dashboard below is fully interactive — use the navigation buttons on the left to switch between the three pages, and the year slicer to filter by period. Best viewed on a desktop screen.

Project details

Dataset
OHID Wider Health Determinants — air pollution (PM2.5, ID 93867) and child poverty (absolute and relative, IDs 93701/93700)
Period covered
2014–2023 (trend analysis) · 2018–2023 (air pollution) · 2022 (latest KPI year)
Geographies
Southampton and statistical neighbours: Portsmouth, Bristol, Plymouth — selected by OHID comparator methodology
Data model
Star schema — Wider_health_determinant_FactTable linked to Area_dimTable, Indicators_dimTable, and Calendar via defined relationships
DAX measures built
8 custom measures: Prior Year values, YoY change labels, RAG status indicators, rank by area, max/min by year for shaded band
Pages
3 pages — Insight Summary (written findings + scatter chart), Overview (KPI cards + trend charts), Indicators Comparisons (matrix + benchmarks)

Key findings

  • Air quality has improved across all four statistical neighbours since 2018. Portsmouth remains the worst performer, sitting above the England average (50th percentile) throughout the period. Plymouth consistently performs best.
  • Southampton's air pollution percentile score of 52 in 2022 places it above the England average, indicating worse-than-average air quality relative to all local authorities in England.
  • Child poverty rates have risen across all four cities since 2019, reversing the improving trend seen between 2014 and 2018. Southampton (18.1%) and Portsmouth (20.8%) both exceed the England average of 17%.
  • A correlation scatter chart confirms a positive relationship between air pollution and child poverty across the peer group — areas with worse air quality also tend to have higher child poverty rates, suggesting the two determinants reinforce each other geographically.
