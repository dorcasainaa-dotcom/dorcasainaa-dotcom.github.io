Wandsworth LBC Performance Dashboard
Revenue Service Expenditure 2021-22 to 2024-25

End-to-end Microsoft Fabric analytics pipeline built on publicly available DLUHC Revenue Outturn RS data for Wandsworth London Borough Council. Four-page Power BI report covering executive KPI overview, service variance analysis, and four-year trend analysis, delivered through a full medallion architecture: Lakehouse, notebook-based ETL, star schema semantic model, and a formatted report with navigation.

DAX measures built

Total Expenditure
Sum of Expenditure_GBP000. Base measure used across all pages and visuals.
Prior Year Expenditure
Calculates expenditure for the prior year using Sort_Order context from Dim_Date.
YoY Change GBP000
Returns blank when multiple years are selected to prevent misleading aggregation.
YoY Change Pct
Year-on-year percentage change. Returns blank unless exactly one year is selected.
People Services Total
CALCULATE filtering Dim_Service category to People Services only.
Place Services Total
CALCULATE filtering Dim_Service category to Place Services only.
Four Year CAGR
Compound Annual Growth Rate using POWER(end/start, 1/3) minus 1. Formatted as percentage.
Pct of Total
Each service as a share of total net expenditure using DIVIDE with ALL() to remove filter context.

Key findings

Adult Social Care: primary cost pressureExpenditure rose from £95m to £132m over four years (+38.3%), driven by demographic demand growth and sustained National Living Wage increases on provider costs. Now the single largest service after Education.
Housing: sharpest proportional riseExpenditure increased from £14.1M to £40.7M, a rise of 187% over four years, reflecting significant temporary accommodation cost pressures on the General Fund.
People Services: 83% of total spendEducation, Adult Social Care and Children Social Care together account for 83% of total net service expenditure in 2024-25. All three are demand-led statutory services with limited scope for reduction.
Highways and Transport: net income positionNegative in all four years, reflecting TfL LIP grant income and parking revenues exceeding gross highway expenditure. This is normal for London Boroughs and reduces overall net spend.
