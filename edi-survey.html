EDI Workforce Analytics Dashboard
Employee Experience and Inclusion Survey

End-to-end people analytics project built on an anonymised employee experience and inclusion survey dataset from a UK higher education institution. The dataset covers 299 unique respondents across six departments and five inclusion dimensions scored on a 1 to 5 Likert scale. The work spans the full analytical lifecycle: data quality assessment and remediation in Power Query, dimensional modelling using a three-table star schema in Power BI Desktop, advanced DAX measures for EDI-specific KPIs, and a seven-page interactive report designed for both technical and senior HR leadership audiences.

Data preparation approach

The raw dataset contained 300 records. The first action in Power Query was to identify and remove a duplicate Employee_ID (E0151), leaving 299 unique respondents. Six Progression values recorded as zero were replaced with null rather than deleted: zero falls outside the valid 1 to 5 response scale and is inconsistent with a skipped or unanswered question. Replacing with null rather than removing the rows preserves those respondents' valid scores across the other four dimensions. All dimension columns were renamed for clean display, a UK locale was applied to correct a date format issue inherited from the source file, and an Age Group Sort column was added as a numeric key to enforce correct chronological ordering in visuals, preventing the default alphabetical sort from misplacing the under-25 group. A composite Inclusion Score column was built using List.Average across the five dimensions so that null Progression values are handled correctly without penalising those respondents.

List.Average of five dimensions. Handles nulls correctly, averaging available values without penalising missing Progression rows.

Step 6
Rename columns

Disability_Status, Manager_Fairness, Age_Group, Speak_Up, Survey_Response_Date renamed for clean visual display.

Data model architecture

The model uses a star schema with three tables and single-direction Many-to-One relationships from the fact table to each dimension. Flat-loading a single denormalised table would not constitute modelling: the star schema separates concerns correctly, enables reliable filter propagation across demographic slicers and drill-throughs, and is scalable if the survey is run annually and new fact rows are appended. All KPIs are built as DAX measures rather than calculated columns, ensuring they evaluate in the correct filter context regardless of which slicer or drill-through is active.

Fact table
FactSurveyResponse

One row per respondent. Holds all five dimension scores, Response Date, Inclusion Score, and Employee_ID as foreign key.

Dimension table
DimEmployee

One row per employee. Holds all demographic attributes: Department, Grade, Gender, Ethnicity, Disability Status, Age Group, Age Group Sort.

Dimension table
DimDate

One row per survey date. Supports year-on-year trend analysis if the survey runs annually. Relationships are Many-to-One, single direction.

Report pages

  • Executive Summary
    Single-view leadership page showing three headline KPI cards, three RAG-coded finding cards covering the progression risk, departmental risks, and positive belonging signal, and three recommended actions with timelines. Designed so a Director can read the full picture in under 30 seconds without navigating to any other page.
  • Overview
    Five KPI cards showing average scores for all inclusion dimensions, a composite Overall Inclusion Score card, a horizontal bar chart of all five dimensions against the 3.5 aspirational target line, and two donut charts showing respondent composition by gender and by ethnicity.
  • Progression
    Deep-dive on the headline risk. Four KPI cards (average progression, percentage negative, gender gap, total respondents), bar charts of average Progression by gender, ethnicity, and age group, and a 100% stacked bar showing the full response distribution (negative, neutral, positive) by gender group.
  • Heatmap
    Four matrix visuals with red-white-green conditional formatting showing all five dimension scores across Ethnicity, Gender, Grade, and Disability Status simultaneously. Department slicer filters all matrices together. A Group Size Label card shows the n= count for any filtered selection and displays a suppression warning automatically when the group drops below ten respondents.
  • Departments
    All five dimension scores shown as a clustered bar chart across all six departments. Dedicated bar charts for Authenticity by department (STEM as lowest) and Speak Up by department (WELS as lowest). Department and Grade slicers with drill-through enabled: right-clicking any department bar on any other page and selecting Drill through navigates to this page filtered to that department.
  • Explore

  • Data Dictionary
    Full governance reference page documenting all Power Query transformation steps, the suppression policy (n less than 10), the non-binary group boundary case, and the data model architecture. Designed so any analyst opening the file can understand every decision made without reading external documentation.

DAX measures built

Total Respondents
COUNTROWS on FactSurveyResponse. Base count measure used across all pages.
Avg Belonging / Authenticity / Manager Fairness / Progression / Speak Up
Five AVERAGE measures on the respective score columns. AVERAGE excludes null values automatically, so the six null Progression rows do not distort the mean.
Overall Inclusion Score
Simple mean of the five dimension averages. Gives leadership one composite headline number for executive comparison across departments and demographic groups.
% Favourable / Neutral / Negative Progression
Three DIVIDE-COUNTROWS-FILTER measures splitting responses into positive (score 4 to 5), neutral (score 3), and negative (score 1 to 2). Used in the 100% stacked bar distribution chart.
Gender Progression Gap
VAR stores Male and Female averages independently using CALCULATE. Returns the arithmetic difference. Positive result confirms men score higher. Used as a KPI card on the Progression page.
Gap vs Target
Avg Progression minus 3.5 (the aspirational benchmark). Returns a negative number when below target. Used in waterfall chart contexts to show how far each dimension falls short.
Dimension Colour (SWITCH)
SWITCH reading the current dimension name from a disconnected Dimensions table and returning the matching average. Enables a single RAG colour measure to drive conditional formatting across all five dimensions in one chart.
Group Size Label
Governance measure: IF COUNTROWS is less than 10, returns a suppression warning. Otherwise returns the n= count. Responds to every slicer and drill-through in real time. Embedded on every breakdown page so the panel always knows the group size behind any filtered view.
Safe Avg Progression
Returns BLANK rather than an average when the filtered group is below the suppression threshold of ten. Used on charts where small-group intersectional combinations could produce statistically unreliable figures.

Key findings

Progression: the most urgent inclusion risk
Progression is the lowest-scoring dimension across the organisation at 2.76 out of 5, with 43% of all respondents rating it negatively. Female respondents average 2.69 versus 2.83 for male respondents, a gap of 0.13 points. 48% of women give a negative score compared to 37% of men. Black employees score the lowest of any ethnic group on Progression at 2.64, compared to 2.75 for White British colleagues. The under-25 age group scores the lowest of any age band (2.62), and the 55 and over group is the second lowest (2.67), indicating the career development pipeline fails people at the start and end of their careers as well as in the middle. These are structural patterns, not individual ones.
STEM: authenticity at 2.74, the lowest department score
STEM records the lowest Authenticity score of any department at 2.74, a gap of 0.63 points below Digital Services at 3.37. Authenticity measures whether staff feel able to bring their full selves to work. In a technical environment where diverse thinking and psychological safety directly affect innovation and problem-solving, a score of 2.74 represents an organisational risk beyond the inclusion dimension alone. This finding warrants a targeted listening session with STEM leadership before any intervention is designed.
WELS: the lowest Speak Up score of any department
WELS records the lowest Speak Up score at 2.96, the only department below 3.0 on this dimension. Speak Up measures whether staff feel able to raise concerns or speak honestly. A score of 2.96 suggests a potential silencing pattern that may reflect management style, team culture, or structural workload pressures. Without a qualitative follow-up, the data cannot identify the cause, and any intervention risks targeting the wrong lever.
Positive signal: Belonging is the strongest dimension for ethnic minority employees
Black employees (3.15) and Asian employees (3.09) report higher Belonging scores than White British colleagues (2.84). This is an important counter-narrative. The problem in this dataset is not hostile or unwelcoming culture: people feel welcomed when they arrive. The barrier is structural progression. This distinction matters for intervention design because the same approach will not fix both things. Diversity and inclusion initiatives focused on welcome and culture will not close a progression gap driven by structural processes such as promotion panels, sponsorship, and stretch opportunity allocation.

Ethical and governance approach

Suppression policy
Groups of fewer than 10 respondents are not reported independently. The non-binary group (n=10) sits exactly at this boundary and is presented as directionally indicative only, with an explicit caveat on every chart it appears in.
Re-identification risk
Intersectional cuts (ethnicity by disability by grade) are suppressed where rare demographic combinations could enable re-identification of individual respondents. Governance is embedded in the report via the Group Size Label measure, not applied manually.
Prefer not to say
Non-disclosure is retained in analysis and treated as an inclusion signal rather than missing data to remove. 15% non-disclosure on ethnicity likely understates ethnic disparity findings: those with most negative experiences may also feel least safe disclosing identity.
Framing principle
All disparities are framed as institutional patterns, not individual characteristics. Language choices matter in EDI reporting: the same finding can prompt action or prompt defensiveness depending on how it is written.
Accessibility
All visuals include alt text. Colour coding is always paired with text labels so the report is usable for colour-blind readers without relying on colour alone to convey meaning.
Feedback loop
Survey participants should be informed of findings and what the organisation commits to doing as a result. Closing this loop is not optional: it is the principal driver of participation rates in future survey cycles.
