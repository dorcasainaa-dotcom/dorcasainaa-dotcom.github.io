EDI Workforce Analytics Dashboard
Employee Experience and Inclusion Survey

End-to-end people analytics project built on an anonymised employee experience and inclusion survey dataset from a UK higher education institution. The dataset covers 299 unique respondents across six departments measuring five inclusion dimensions on a 1 to 5 Likert scale, modelled using a star schema and reported across seven interactive pages designed for both technical and senior HR leadership audiences.

Built on an anonymised survey dataset of 299 respondents. All outputs are aggregate. No individual-level data is included in this portfolio.

Architecture
Data preparation and model

The raw dataset contained 300 records. One duplicate Employee_ID was removed, leaving 299 unique respondents. Six Progression values recorded as zero were replaced with null rather than deleted: zero falls outside the valid 1 to 5 scale and is inconsistent with a skipped question, and replacing with null preserves those respondents' valid scores across the other four dimensions. A UK locale was applied to correct a date parsing error in the source file, and an Age Group Sort column was added as a numeric key to enforce correct chronological ordering, preventing the default alphabetical sort from misplacing the under-25 group. A composite Inclusion Score was calculated using List.Average across the five dimensions so that null Progression values are averaged correctly without penalising those respondents.

Data model
Star schema

Three tables: FactSurveyResponse (scores and date), DimEmployee (all demographic attributes), DimDate (survey dates). Many-to-One, single-direction relationships.

Power Query
Cleaning decisions

Duplicate removed. Six Progression zeros replaced with null. UK date locale applied. Age Group Sort column added. Columns renamed for display.

DAX measures
Measures table

All KPIs built as DAX measures in a dedicated _Measures table, not as calculated columns, ensuring correct filter context behaviour across all slicers and drill-throughs.

Governance
Suppression built in

Group Size Label measure evaluates COUNTROWS in the current filter context and returns a suppression warning automatically when any filtered group drops below ten respondents.

Report
Report pages
  • Executive Summary
    Single-view leadership page with three headline KPI cards, three RAG-coded finding cards covering the progression risk, departmental risks, and the positive Belonging signal, and three recommended actions with timelines. Designed for a Director to read the full picture without navigating to any other page.
  • Overview
    Five KPI cards for all inclusion dimensions, an Overall Inclusion Score card, a horizontal bar chart of all five dimensions against the 3.5 aspirational target line, and two donut charts showing respondent composition by gender and ethnicity.
  • Progression
    Deep-dive on the headline risk. Four KPI cards, bar charts of average Progression by gender, ethnicity, and age group, and a 100% stacked bar showing the full response distribution (negative, neutral, positive) by gender group.
  • Heatmap
    Four matrix visuals with red-white-green conditional formatting showing all five dimension scores across Ethnicity, Gender, Grade, and Disability Status simultaneously. A department slicer filters all matrices together. The Group Size Label card shows the n= for any filtered selection and suppresses automatically when a group drops below ten.
  • Departments
    All five dimension scores as a clustered bar chart across all six departments, with dedicated charts for Authenticity by department (STEM lowest) and Speak Up by department (WELS lowest). Drill-through enabled: right-clicking any department bar on any other page navigates to this page filtered to that department.
  • Explore
    Two Decomposition Tree visuals for live Q&A use: one analysing Avg Progression by Department, Gender, Ethnicity, and Grade; one analysing Avg Belonging to surface the positive counter-narrative. Used interactively during the presentation rather than pre-configured.
  • Data Dictionary
    Full governance reference page documenting every Power Query transformation step, the suppression policy, the non-binary group boundary case, and the data model architecture, so any analyst opening the file can understand every decision without reading external documentation.
DAX
DAX measures built
Total Respondents
COUNTROWS on FactSurveyResponse. Base count used across all pages.
Avg Belonging / Authenticity / Manager Fairness / Progression / Speak Up
Five AVERAGE measures. AVERAGE excludes null values automatically so the six null Progression rows do not distort the mean.
Overall Inclusion Score
Mean of the five dimension averages. One composite headline number for executive comparison across departments and demographic groups.
% Favourable / Neutral / Negative Progression
Three DIVIDE-COUNTROWS-FILTER measures splitting responses into positive (4 to 5), neutral (3), and negative (1 to 2). Used in the 100% stacked bar chart.
Gender Progression Gap
VAR stores Male and Female averages independently via CALCULATE, then returns the arithmetic difference. A positive result confirms men score higher.
Dimension Colour (SWITCH)
SWITCH reads the current dimension name from a disconnected Dimensions table and returns the matching average, driving conditional formatting across all five dimensions in one chart.
Group Size Label
Governance measure: IF COUNTROWS is below ten, returns a suppression warning. Otherwise returns the n= count. Responds to every slicer and drill-through in real time.
Safe Avg Progression
Returns BLANK rather than an average when the filtered group is below the suppression threshold of ten. Used on charts where intersectional combinations risk producing unreliable figures.
Analysis
Key findings
Progression: the most urgent inclusion risk
Progression is the lowest-scoring dimension at 2.76 out of 5, with 43% of all respondents rating it negatively. Female respondents average 2.69 versus 2.83 for male respondents, and 48% of women give a negative score compared to 37% of men. Black employees score the lowest of any ethnic group at 2.64, compared to 2.75 for White British colleagues. The under-25 age group scores lowest of any age band (2.62), and the 55 and over group is second lowest (2.67): the career development pipeline fails people at both ends as well as in the middle. These are structural patterns, not individual ones.
STEM: Authenticity at 2.74, the lowest department score
STEM records the lowest Authenticity score of any department at 2.74, a gap of 0.63 points below Digital Services at 3.37. In a technical environment where diverse thinking and psychological safety directly affect innovation and problem-solving, a score of 2.74 represents an organisational risk beyond the inclusion dimension alone.
WELS: the lowest Speak Up score of any department
WELS records the lowest Speak Up score at 2.96, the only department below 3.0 on this dimension. Whether this reflects management style, team culture, or structural workload pressures, the data cannot identify the cause without a qualitative follow-up, and any intervention risks targeting the wrong lever without one.
Positive signal: Belonging is strongest for ethnic minority employees
Black employees (3.15) and Asian employees (3.09) report higher Belonging scores than White British colleagues (2.84). The problem in this dataset is not hostile or unwelcoming culture: people feel welcomed when they arrive. The barrier is structural progression. This distinction matters for intervention design because the same approach will not fix both problems.
Governance
Ethical and governance approach
Suppression policy
Groups of fewer than 10 respondents are not reported independently. The non-binary group (n=10) sits exactly at this boundary and is presented as directionally indicative only, with an explicit caveat on every chart it appears in.
Re-identification risk
Intersectional cuts are suppressed where rare demographic combinations could enable re-identification. Governance is embedded via the Group Size Label measure, not applied manually after the fact.
Prefer not to say
Non-disclosure is retained and treated as an inclusion signal rather than missing data. 15% non-disclosure on ethnicity likely understates ethnic disparity findings.
Framing
All disparities are framed as institutional patterns, not individual characteristics. Language choices matter in EDI reporting: the same finding can prompt action or prompt defensiveness depending on how it is written.
Accessibility
All visuals include alt text. Colour coding is always paired with text labels so the report is usable for colour-blind readers without relying on colour alone to convey meaning.
Feedback loop
Survey participants should be informed of findings and what the organisation commits to doing. Closing this loop is the principal driver of participation rates in future survey cycles.
